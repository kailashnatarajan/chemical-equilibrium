Chemical Equilibrium & Le Chatelier's Principle

Explore how dynamic equilibrium responds to changes in temperature, pressure, and concentration. This virtual lab lets you manipulate variables in the Haber Process and observe real-time effects on NH₃ yield — a cornerstone of industrial chemistry.

⚖️ What is Chemical Equilibrium?

Chemical equilibrium is a dynamic state in a reversible reaction where the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction. At this point, the concentrations of reactants and products remain constant over time — though both reactions continue to occur simultaneously.

🔄 Le Chatelier's Principle

"If a system at equilibrium is subjected to a change in concentration, temperature, pressure, or volume, the system will shift its equilibrium position to oppose that change and establish a new equilibrium."

Proposed by Henri Louis Le Chatelier in 1884.

📌 Equilibrium Constant (Kc)

For a reaction aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD:

Kc = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ / [A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ

Kc depends only on temperature. If Q < Kc → forward shift; Q > Kc → reverse shift; Q = Kc → equilibrium.

🌡️ Effect of Temperature

  • Exothermic reaction: ↑T → shift left (less product)
  • Exothermic reaction: ↓T → shift right (more product)
  • Endothermic reaction: ↑T → shift right (more product)
  • Temperature changes Kc value

🔧 Effect of Pressure

  • ↑Pressure → shifts to side with fewer gas moles
  • ↓Pressure → shifts to side with more gas moles
  • N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃: 4 moles → 2 moles
  • ↑P favors NH₃ formation (product side)

⚗️ Effect of Concentration

  • ↑[reactant] → shifts forward (more product)
  • ↑[product] → shifts backward (more reactant)
  • Catalyst: speeds up both reactions equally
  • Catalyst does NOT shift equilibrium

📚 Key Takeaways
  • Le Chatelier's Principle allows us to predict equilibrium shifts when conditions change.
  • For the exothermic Haber Process, low temperature favors NH₃ but is kinetically too slow industrially.
  • High pressure (150–200 atm) is used industrially to maximize NH₃ yield per pass.
  • Kc depends only on temperature — increasing T decreases Kc for exothermic reactions.
  • The comparison of Q vs Kc determines the direction of spontaneous equilibrium shift.
  • A catalyst (iron with promoters) is used to speed equilibrium attainment without shifting it.
